A core idea behind the current liberal balance is that individuals and corporations are free to pursue their own self-interested profit maximization, but that the government is there to step in when the profit incentives get too far out of line with human flourishing.

During most of the 20th century the main tools were trust breaking, anti-monopoly laws, and labor unions.1 This worked for a time, but there are genuine reasons to want monopolies or near monopolies. The economies of scale they unlock provide great value to all of us, and for many sectors (utilities, rail, social media) competition is infeasible. In the end for forces of efficeny won out. As for labor unions… put a pin in that.

After that the order of the day became regulation and market incentives:

Regulations are the most straightforward approach, and necessary2 for fixing many kinds of externalities (building safety codes, ect). But, we are living in a world of increasing globalization, and I want my bank to work everywhere! Like with monopolies before, allowing for trans nationals comes with all sorts of benefits, and territory by territory regulations makes it hard if not impossible to for them to operate.

In addtion, many of the things we would like to regulate, such as environmental impact, have been undermined by the abiltiy to outsource to different countries.3 The Internet was the straw the broke the camel's back. Already regulation was failing to keep up with the pace of inovation, now large tech companies are able to act essentially unfetered by any goverment oversight.

Market Incentives attempt to more directly modify the profit incentive to put it directly in line with our values. The problem is that as multinationals have grown increasingly powerful, so has the ability to subvert democracy[not a new problem] and replace the people's will with their own.

This looks like Cambridge Analytica using massive amount's of data to manipulte peopls opinions to install convinent leaders, and using their vast hordes of money to "generous campaign donations". As bad as things are in the US, the global south is almost entirely at the mercy of the interest of foreign firms with no respect for their national sovereignty, at the mercy of the market incentives put in place by the north.

The reason we need to regulate and control companies in the first place is because the profit incentive is not well enough aligned with our own human values. If we allow the goverment in turn to be manipulted by the profit incentive we optimize towards using corn to make ethinol!

Democracy is the best system we’ve found for aligning the action of organization with the genuine interest of the people (although obviously not perfectly so). So what if we just skipped the middle man and made our corporations democracies?

To assuage his fears, my cousin's boot company has no reason to take outside input if he doesn't want it to. The need for democratic accountability grows in proportion to how inescapable a body is. If you don't like his boots, just go buy some others. MasterCard has no such luxury. Consumers have no meaningful choice whether to use them or not.

Labor Unions are an attempt at introducing some degree of democratic oversight into the process, but they fail on two counts:

  1. Even at their best they only turn companies into worker coops, leaving consumers unrepresented.
  2. For a unions to work well they require very active and engaged workers. This is in part due to the combative nature of labor unions -- you have to be motivated to go on strike, but it's also a natural consiquence of the voting structure.

The current paradigm, as much as one exists, is to work on international treaties and organizations capable of controlling the behavior of companies across the world, either via regulations or market incentives (ex: EU, UN, ...[more examples here]). Schemes that some imagine might turn into the one world government of the future (and some imagine already have!).

One thing that draws me to anarchism/libertarianism is less so the ability to refuse to participate in systems you don't like, but the possibilities of new structures to organically evolve and grow without the need for approval from their powers that be. Imagine if you could opt out of your government as easily as you could opt out of google? It's still very hard to do (I've tried and failed), but achievable to those committed to the idea.

Perhaps Amazon could implement a loto-acracy with a random sample of users of their services. Maybe Microsoft wants to implement liquid democracy across all those who pay for licenses. Meta could elect 5 year emperors. And X Ai... trial by combat?

Of course for this to really take route would require legal compulsion. And indeed, I think it would be a good idea to force trans nationals or other giant companies to start incorporating at least some degree of representatives in a relatively unconstrained way. We don't have a lot of data both on the performance of Coops vs traditional boards for large companies, but the limited data we do have suggest they compare favorably[citation needed].

We are living in unprecedented times, two of the companies set to take over the world are respectively a non profit* and a public benefit corporation. Either one of whom could realistically start to transition into a democracy of its own volition. I suspect Anthropic is more open to this idea, so the rest of this blog post will be aimed at them, but OpenAI, or to a lesser extent other frontier labs, could easily be substituted instead.

But first, let’s talk about the role AI plays in all this.

At least at first Anthropic wouldn't need to actually take the suggestions returned by this process as they work .

Some open questions: is it better to ask as many people as possible to have a brief conversation with Claude, or have fewer people have deeper conversations to get more of their informed preference. If you do both, how do you weigh between them? How do you factor in the sampling biases inherent to who is interested in this kind of experiment?

I think this is especially interesting when Max Harms works on corrigibility. To be *very* brief: he suggested we drop Harmless & Honest to focus 100% on Helpful. One important aspect of any corrigible agent is an open mindedness and curiosity about the preferences of the one it's helping. In this case the agent would be curious about the democratic will of the people.

The major danger of corrigible agents is power concentration and _____. If we want are AIs to be aligned ASI

In practice, how might this look?

Phase 1: Users are asked if they would like to answer some questions from the LLM about what direction Anthropic should be taking. This could be done on a rolling basis both with questions about a general roadmap for the future, as well as questions on more specific decisions that the company is facing. The main goal is to start putting in place the process and gather useful feedback that can be used by the internal team to inform their decisions.

Phase 2: Once we have a scaffold in place that can assess users preferences on decisions, it's time to test it out on smaller decisions. For example, edge cases around allowed content policies or _____. The first couple of times the entire process should be carefully examined to see what can be learned from it, and to make sure no major democratic misalignment is found, but after that employees would hopefully start routinely giving over minor decisions to the Demos.

Phase 3: From there, as Claude gets more powerful and more capable of making smart decisions, more power would be gradually handed to it. Perhaps the democratic system would get a seat on the board? Ideally some more traditional democratic schemes (like direct voting/polling) would also be put in place as a sanity check on the output.

The risk of such a scheme is that it involves empowering the LLM to make more decisions, making misaligned AI even more dangerous. But if ASI is misaligned, we're dead anyways, so it’s somewhat of a moot point.

In an incredible world where all AI labs agree/are forced to be open to direct democratic input, it could also provide a new route to an AI Pause, but I consider this very unlikely.
